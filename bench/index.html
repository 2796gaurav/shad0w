decision bench · six public tasks · CPU only

Same rows. Same certifier.
Microseconds against seconds.

Five open decision models (Julia-1, Laya, Kev-0.8B, Decider-0.8B, GLiNER2.5-Decide) and shad0w answer the same test rows of six public tasks, one at a time on a laptop CPU, through the same 5% certificate. shad0w was trained on each task; the decision models answered zero-shot. Every answer is saved.

shad0w comes in two forms: linear (one hashed table) and deep (a small hidden layer on top). auto keeps whichever certifies more, and that is the row called shad0w. Decision models answer in milliseconds to seconds; the table in microseconds.

What it shows, and what it doesn't.

≠Different training

shad0w learns each task from its training split; the decision models answer zero-shot. A decision model fine-tuned on the task would score higher than here.

✓Labels as the teacher

Here the teacher is the dataset's labels. In your app shad0w learns from your LLM's answers, and its certificate is against that LLM, not the truth.

1One machine, batch size 1

Decision-model times include localhost HTTP and are on a CPU. They would be lower in-process, and much lower on a GPU (llama.cpp publishes 3–12 ms there).

  • Rows. 500 test rows per task, a fixed random sample of the test split; 500 calibration rows from the training split, held out from training. A model that takes over 1 s per decision answers the first 150 rows, marked "150 rows".
  • Certified uses Learn-then-Test with a Clopper–Pearson bound (α = 5%, δ = 10%) for every system: a confidence threshold picked on the calibration rows. Coverage is the share of test rows at or above it; served error is the error on those rows. A system whose confidence cannot separate right from wrong certifies 0%.
  • Time is the wall time of one call with one input, after 20 warm-up calls. shad0w runs in-process on one thread through the C core. Decision models run with 5 threads; their single-thread time is shown where it was measured.
  • Left out: Laya-multilingual, Laya-typed-decisions and d1-omni-600M did not load in this llama.cpp build. The 4B–9B models take seconds per decision on a CPU and are optional in the harness.
