how it works

Learn. Certify. Serve.

Your LLM keeps answering. shad0w writes the answers down, learns a small table from them, proves where it agrees, and answers the repeats.

01

Learn

About 1,000 logged answers become a table. No labels, no GPU, under a minute.

02

Certify

On answers it never trained on, it finds the confidence above which it differs from your LLM on at most 5%, at 90% confidence.

03

Serve

Above that line the table answers in microseconds. Below it your LLM answers, and the table keeps learning. 1% is spot-checked live.

tablethe small thing shad0w learns. It ships in a folder, the bundle.
teacherthe model it learns from: your LLM or a decision model.
certifiedtested on answers it never trained on; differs from the teacher on at most α of what it serves, 90% confidence.
deferthe table is not sure, so this one goes to the teacher and is logged.
αthe disagreement you allow. Default 5%: at most 1 in 20.

integrate

One line, or zero.

Wrap the question you already ask, or point base_url at the proxy.

pythonsupport_router.py
import shad0w

intent = shad0w.decision("intent", llm="openai/gpt-4.1-mini",
    options=["refund", "lost_card", "balance", "other"])

intent("my card was stolen").answer   # your LLM answers; shad0w logs it
intent.train()                        # after ~1,000 answers: learn + certify
intent("my card was stolen")          # Decision(answer='lost_card', source='table')

Any OpenAI-compatible model, the OpenAI Decisions API, a decision model like Jev, or your own function: shad0w.Shadow("bundle/", llm=my_fn). Async: await intent.adecide(text).

javascriptroute.mjs
import { decision } from "shad0wllm";

const intent = await decision("intent", {
  options: ["refund", "lost_card", "balance", "other"],
  llm: "openai/gpt-4.1-mini", bundle: "shad0w/intent/bundle" });

const d = await intent.decide("my card was stolen");
// { answer: "lost_card", source: "table", certified: true }

Is this just a cache?

No. A cache only answers texts it has seen before. shad0w answers new wordings of a question it has learned, and its certificate covers new messages that look like your traffic. Exact repeats are free on top.

What does "certified" mean?

Before it answers anything, the table is tested on answers it never trained on and picks a confidence line. Above it, the table differs from your LLM on at most α of what it answers, checked with 90% confidence. α is the most disagreement you allow: the default 5% means at most 1 in 20 answers. In our runs it actually differed 3.0–6.0% of the time: a 90% promise can miss, so live spot checks keep watching.

What if my LLM is wrong?

Then the table is wrong in the same way. The certificate is agreement with your LLM, not with the truth. For a bound against the truth, certify on a few hundred human-labelled examples instead.

How many examples does it need, and what does it cost?

About 1,000 logged answers per question to start; with 77 intents it answered 60% at 10,000 answers, and the share keeps growing. It costs nothing: Apache-2.0, the Python runtime needs only numpy, the JavaScript and C runtimes need nothing, and your logs stay on your machine.
