Let a table do the next one.

An LLM web agent bought things on a small shop website 300 times, one click or keystroke per step, about 2 seconds a step. shad0w learned its choices from the pages' HTML. Now the table runs the same errand in microseconds a step, and hands the step back to the LLM when it isn't certified, when the page has something it has never seen, or when its last action changed nothing. No screenshots: the agent only reads HTML.

where it doesn't work
New websites are the LLM's job.

      
We also tried the hard part of computer use: real websites the table had never seen, from the public Mind2Web dataset (… tasks on … websites, ~… clickable elements per step).

    

    
    
      
  • Which element to click, read from each element's tag, attributes and text: right first time on … of steps for new tasks on known sites and … on unseen sites (a simple word-overlap rule gets …). The certificate refused to serve any of it, even with a 20% error budget. That refusal is correct.
    • 
      
  • What kind of page is this (search results, form, product, …), labelled by an LLM: the table agreed with it on … of pages from known sites but only … on unseen sites. On unseen sites its certified answers missed their error budget (on a small sample): a certificate holds for pages like the ones it was tested on, not for a new website.
    • 
    

  


  

    
what this means
Record once with your LLM. Replay at table speed.

    
    
      
  • Works: a repeated workflow on a site you know (RPA-style errands, back-office forms, test scripts). The LLM teaches; the table replays in microseconds a step and passes anything unusual back.
    • 
      
  • Doesn't: finding its way on websites it hasn't learned. Keep a big model, or a person, for that.
    • 
      
  • The certificate is not enough on its own. It promises low disagreement on pages like the ones it was tested on. When the site changes, the table can be wrong and certain. Always pair it with the two cheap checks on this page, and keep irreversible actions (paying, deleting, sending) behind the LLM or a human.
    • 
      
  • One synthetic shop is a small test. The LLM made no mistakes here, so this shows a table copying a reliable agent, not fixing a bad one.
    •
