Monitoring#

You should be able to see, at any moment, how much the table is answering, how fast, why it deferred, and whether it still agrees with your LLM. shad0w gives you that out of the box.

The dashboard#

shad0w proxy and shad0w serve both serve a live page at /:

shad0w proxy --upstream https://api.openai.com/v1 --cost-per-call 0.0006
open http://localhost:8010

It shows, updated every second:

  • share answered by the table (with a sparkline);
  • LLM calls saved, time saved (calls saved × your LLM's measured mean latency, or --llm-latency-ms until one is measured) and money saved (with --cost-per-call);
  • per question: table vs LLM latency (p50), why the table deferred (not sure, drift, not trained, and the rollout reasons: shadow, canary, never_serve, min_confidence), and the live safety bound;
  • the latest decisions, with source, confidence and latency (--no-text hides the text).

The live safety bound is the 90% upper confidence bound on disagreement among table answers, computed from background spot checks (audit_rate, default 1%). While it is at or below α the dashboard shows it in green; above α it turns amber and says "re-train".

In a terminal:

shad0w watch --url http://localhost:8010

Prometheus#

GET /metrics:

shad0w_decisions_total{question="intent",source="table"} 8123
shad0w_decisions_total{question="intent",source="teacher"} 3877
shad0w_flags_total{question="intent",flag="low_confidence"} 3790
shad0w_offload_ratio{question="intent"} 0.677
shad0w_audit_disagreement_upper{question="intent"} 0.041
shad0w_alpha{question="intent"} 0.05
shad0w_llm_calls_saved_total 8123
shad0w_latency_seconds_bucket{question="intent",source="table",le="2.5e-05"} 8090
...

Alert on shad0w_audit_disagreement_upper > shad0w_alpha, and on a falling shad0w_offload_ratio (traffic changed).

In your own app, share one Metrics object:

metrics = shad0w.Metrics(cost_per_call=0.0006)
intent  = shad0w.decision("intent", ..., metrics=metrics)
urgency = shad0w.decision("urgent", ..., metrics=metrics)
metrics.prometheus()      # text for your /metrics route
metrics.snapshot()        # the same data as a dict

Logs#

SHAD0W_LOG=debug python app.py

14:02:11 shad0w DEBUG intent -> 'lost_card' via table conf=0.993 flag=None 9.4us | my card was stolen
14:02:11 shad0w DEBUG intent -> 'other' via teacher conf=0.41 flag=low_confidence 612034.2us | explain interest
14:05:40 shad0w INFO trained 'intent' on 1204 answers in 3.9s: certified share 68.2% at alpha=0.05

SHAD0W_LOG=info shows only training, reloads and spot-check disagreements. Or attach your own handler to the shad0w logger.

Hooks and traces#

def send(decision, text):            # Langfuse, Datadog, Sentry, your warehouse...
    analytics.track("shad0w", {"answer": decision.answer, "source": decision.source,
                               "us": decision.latency_us, "flag": decision.flag})

intent = shad0w.decision("intent", ..., on_decision=send, trace="decisions.jsonl")

trace= writes every decision (table and LLM) as one JSON line. It is separate from the training log on purpose: table answers never become training data.

OpenTelemetry#

pip install opentelemetry-api opentelemetry-sdk
SHAD0W_OTEL=1 python app.py

Each decision becomes a span named shad0w.decide with attributes:

  • shad0w.question, shad0w.source, shad0w.answer, shad0w.confidence, shad0w.certified, shad0w.flag;
  • gen_ai.request.model, the teacher.

Playground#

The dashboard has a Playground card: pick a question, type a text, and see the table's answer, its confidence against the certified threshold, the reason in words and the top options. On shad0w proxy started with --model, it can also ask your LLM for the same text and show both answers side by side. Nothing typed there is logged unless you ask for it. The same is available as POST /v1/playground (see HTTP).

Explain one decision#

shad0w try --bundle shad0w/intent/bundle "the ATM ate my card"

the ATM ate my card
  ✓ table  card_swallowed  (confidence 0.962, needs 0.911)  10.2 µs
     certified: answered by the table
     top: card_swallowed 0.96, lost_card 0.03, other 0.01

The same from Python: intent.explain(text).
