Limits#

shad0w is careful engineering on well-known ideas: a fastText-shaped table and a Learn-then-Test certificate. Knowing where it stops is what makes the guarantee useful.

It picks; it doesn't write#

One answer from a fixed list per question. Chat, summaries, extraction and reasoning stay with your LLM.

Surface wording, not meaning#

The table learns from words and character patterns. It is excellent at intents, routes, topics and policies with distinctive wording. It is poor at:

  • sentiment, tone, sarcasm and emotion: about 79% on SST-2 even with perfect labels;
  • world knowledge and reasoning: "is this claim true?";
  • long documents where one clause decides: legal clause types reached only 13% offload in our tests.

In these cases shad0w certifies little or nothing and passes the traffic to your LLM. That is safe, but it saves you nothing.

The guarantee is against your LLM#

A certified table in front of a wrong LLM is wrong the same way. For a bound against the truth, calibrate on ~300 human labels.

It assumes traffic like the calibration traffic#

A big change in what users send can break the bound. The certificate is computed on a slice of your logged traffic, so it describes traffic with that same mix.

The most common way this goes wrong is the share of off-topic messages. In our CLINC150 run, the logged traffic had very few out-of-scope messages and the test traffic had many more. The table was certified on the first mix and served the second, and its realised disagreement went above α. Messages it should have sent to other were sometimes answered with a real intent. See where shad0w loses.

What protects you:

  • Log real traffic, including the off-topic part, at its real rate. Don't filter the log down to "good" examples before training.
  • Always offer an other option, so the LLM has somewhere to put messages that fit no intent and the table learns that answer too. If answering a real intent by mistake is costly, add never_serve = ["other"] so off-topic calls always go to your LLM.
  • Watch the drift guard. It notices when the share of low-confidence inputs rises above what calibration saw. That is what a jump in off-topic traffic looks like. It then marks answers drift and sends them to your LLM until the mix settles or you retrain. Spot checks catch slower shifts.
  • Retrain when the mix changes, for example after a launch or a new channel.

Most accurate answer, every time?#

Then call your best model. A 9B decision model scored 7 points higher on BANKING77 than the table, at about 70,000× the latency. shad0w belongs in front of that model, not instead of it.

No built-in auth#

shad0w serve and shad0w proxy have no authentication. Run them on localhost or behind your own gateway.
