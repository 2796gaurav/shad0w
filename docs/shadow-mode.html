Shadow mode, step by step#

Shadow mode is the main way to use shad0w. Your model keeps doing its job, and shad0w learns from its answers. No human labels are involved at any point.

1. Pick a decision that compiles#

Good fits are decisions where the words carry the answer:

Decision Typical options Expected fit
Customer intent in support or banking 20–150 strong: 45–71% offloaded at α = 5% in tests
Ticket or email queue routing 5–50 strong when queues have distinctive vocabulary
Topic and category labelling 4–100 strong on news and encyclopedic topics
Spam, scam, prompt-injection screening 2–5 good when the classes have distinctive wording; test on your data
Agent step: allow / block / escalate 2–5 depends on how consistent your model is; the certificate tells you
Sentiment, tone, emotion 2–6 poor: expect near-zero offload, keep the model
Anything needing reasoning or world knowledge — out of scope

You don't have to guess. If a decision does not compile, the certificate says so (certified share ≈ 0%) and nothing is served.

2. Write the schema#

{"intent": {"type": "choice", "criteria": {"refund": "money back", "lost_card": "card lost or stolen", "balance": null, "other": null}}}
  • Option names must match your model's answers exactly.
  • If your model can say "none of these", include that option (for example other), so the table learns it too.
  • A yesno question takes true/false answers.

shad0w init writes a starter file.

3. Log your model's answers#

The simplest way is the wrapper. With no bundle, every call goes to your model and is logged:

import shad0w

def ask_my_llm(text: str) -> str:
    ...  # your existing call; must return one of the schema's option names

sh = shad0w.Shadow(None, teacher=ask_my_llm, question="intent", log="teacher_log.jsonl")
answer = sh.decide(user_message).answer

You can also write the JSON Lines file yourself. Each line has text and one field per question:

{"text": "my card was stolen yesterday", "intent": "lost_card"}

How much?

  • At least 100 answers per question to run.
  • About 1,000 for a useful certificate.
  • 10k–100k for high offload at tight α. On AG News, offload at α = 5% grew from 31% at 2k answers to 70% at 100k.

Log representative traffic, not a curated sample. The certificate is only valid for traffic like it.

4. Compile and certify#

shad0w shadow --schema schema.json --data teacher_log.jsonl --teacher my-llm-v3 --alpha 0.05 --out bundle/

compiled and certified ['intent'] in 74.2s -> bundle/
  intent: agreement with teacher 85.3%; certified share 71.2% at alpha=0.05

What happened:

  1. The log was split. 30% of it (at most 3,000 records) was set aside for calibration and never used for fitting.
  2. A hashed n-gram table was fitted on the rest.
  3. On the calibration slice, Learn-then-Test picked the lowest confidence threshold at which disagreement with your model is still provably ≤ α.

5. Read the certificate#

shad0w report --bundle bundle/

{
  "format": "shad0w-certificate/1", "certified_against": "teacher", "teacher": "my-llm-v3",
  "alpha": 0.05, "delta": 0.1,
  "statement": "On inputs drawn like the calibration slice, among answers this bundle serves with certified=true, the rate of disagreement with the teacher is at most alpha with probability at least 1 - delta. ...",
  "questions": {"intent": {
    "n_records": 10000, "n_fit": 7000, "n_calibration": 3000, "data_sha256": "9f2c…",
    "agreement_with_teacher": 0.853, "threshold": 0.91,
    "certified_share_on_calibration": 0.712, "disagreement_on_certified_calibration": 0.041}}
}
Field Read it as
agreement_with_teacher how often the table matches your model on all calibration traffic
certified_share_on_calibration the share the table will serve itself (your offload)
disagreement_on_certified_calibration the observed disagreement among served answers; the bound α sits above it
threshold the certified confidence cut-off; null means nothing is certified
data_sha256 a hash of the exact records used, for audit trails (the records themselves are never stored)

A certified share of 0% is a correct result, not a bug. It means the bound is out of reach for this decision at this α with this much data. Collect more data, loosen α, or keep the decision on your model.

6. Serve#

sh = shad0w.Shadow("bundle/", teacher=ask_my_llm, log="teacher_log.jsonl", audit_rate=0.01)
d = sh.decide(user_message)
  • d.source == "table": certified, served in microseconds.
  • d.source == "teacher": deferred. d.flag says why: low_confidence, low_radius (with exposed=True), or drift. The answer was logged.
  • With probability audit_rate, a certified answer is also sent to your model, as a spot check. sh.stats() reports offload, audit disagreement and a Clopper–Pearson upper bound on it.
sh.stats()
# {'table': 7104, 'teacher': 2896, 'audits': 71, 'audit_disagreements': 2,
#  'offload': 0.71, 'audit_disagreement': 0.028, 'audit_disagreement_upper': 0.072}

Other runtimes (JavaScript, HTTP, C) are in Integrations.

7. Keep it honest over time#

Do When How
Re-certify weekly, after a product launch, when drift fires shad0w certify --bundle bundle/ --data last_week.jsonl (records not used to compile)
Recompile monthly, or when you want more offload shad0w shadow ... on the accumulated log
Watch audits continuously sh.stats(); for anytime-valid monitoring, shad0w.audit.Auditor
Restart the log when your model changes the certificate names the teacher it was computed against

Hot-swap without restarting: sh.reload("bundle-v2/").

Exposed channels. If adversaries can probe the input (public forms, chat), pass exposed=True. Answers whose exact robustness radius is below r_min inserted items are then deferred.
