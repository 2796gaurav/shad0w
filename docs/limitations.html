Limitations#

Every item here is measured, with what happens and what to do about it. Knowing the limits is what makes the certificate useful.

1. The certificate is about agreement with your model, not the truth#

The bound says how often the table disagrees with your model on the answers it serves. If your model is wrong, the table is certified to be wrong in the same way. In tests, cascade accuracy tracked the teacher's closely, from 29% to 86% depending on the task.

Do: pick a teacher you trust for this decision. For a bound against the truth, calibrate with ~300 real labels per question (shad0w calibrate).

2. The bound holds on traffic like the calibration slice#

Learn-then-Test bounds are marginal over the calibration distribution. On full test sets at α = 5%, realised disagreement was 3.9–5.7% (mean of three seeds), and the worst single seed reached 6.5% on a 600-item test set. On a test slice dominated by one class, it ran 6–7% against 5%.

Do:

  • Calibrate on representative traffic.
  • Re-certify on a schedule and whenever drift fires.
  • Keep the Shadow wrapper's audits on (1% default).
  • Never describe the bound as a guarantee of correctness.

3. Sentiment, tone and emotion are out of scope#

With perfect labels, the hashed table reaches ~79% on SST‑2, against 90.5% for a 300M encoder. In shadow mode it certifies 0–5% of such traffic and defers the rest.

Do: keep those questions on your model.

4. Not the most accurate engine#

On the public local-jev-bench BANKING77 file, a 9B decision model scores 97% against the table's 89.7%.

Do: put the table in front of the accurate model, not instead of it.

5. It needs shadow data#

A useful certificate needs about 1,000 teacher answers per question. High offload at tight α needs tens of thousands: 52% at α = 2% on AG News took 100k.

Do: start logging on day one with Shadow(None, ...). Offload grows without code changes.

6. Out-of-scope and adversarial input#

  • Out-of-scope: confidence separates it well (AUROC 0.94 on CLINC150), but not perfectly. At a 0.8 threshold, 8.5% of out-of-scope queries were still answered. Add an explicit other option if your model has one.
  • Insertion attacks: short texts can be flipped by inserting a few words. The exact radius and exposed=True identify fragile answers and defer them; they cannot make them robust.

7. Small size budgets need few options#

256 KB keeps 88.5% for 77 options but falls to 85.1% for 150 options, and 128 KB collapses for 150 options. The K × K radius matrix alone takes 4K² bytes.

8. Languages#

Hashing is byte-level and works in any script. Quality depends on your teacher in shadow mode. Romanised Hindi–English worked with human labels (84.7%, 2.7 points below English).

Do: test on your own traffic first.

9. The reference HTTP server#

shad0w serve is a threaded standard-library server. Its p99 rises to 139 ms at 128 concurrent clients, and it has no authentication.

Do: run in-process, or put it behind your own front end.

10. Not new science#

The certificate is Learn-then-Test, in the spirit of Trust or Escalate and BARGAIN. The table is fastText-shaped. The contribution is the measured, packaged combination: a teacher-agreement certificate delivered as a small CPU artifact, with identical runtimes in three languages and a one-line integration.
