Tuning & fine-tuning#

shad0w has one number that decides safety (alpha) and a handful of knobs that decide how much it answers. This page is a set of recipes. Every setting can be passed in code, as a SHAD0W_* variable, or in shad0w.toml (Configuration).

"It answers too little"#

The share the table answers comes from how consistent your teacher is and how much of your traffic it has seen. In order of impact:

  1. Log more traffic. The certified share grows with logged answers. On a 77-option task nothing was certified below about 5,000 answers; on 8 options, about 1,000 was enough. See Benchmarks.
  2. Offer an other option and log off-topic traffic at its real rate. Without it, off-topic messages get forced into real options, the table gets less sure everywhere, and the certificate has to be stricter.
  3. Make the teacher consistent. Temperature 0, a fixed prompt, and option descriptions that do not overlap. A teacher that answers the same message differently on two calls caps what any student can certify.
  4. Raise alpha if your product tolerates it. 5% suits routing and triage. Each step up moves the threshold down and serves more. Measure on your own log first: shad0w train --alpha 0.1 prints the share you would get.
  5. Merge options your teacher confuses. If card_lost and card_stolen lead to the same action, one option serves more and loses nothing. Use rename to merge them without losing history (Changing your options).
  6. Turn on spot checks early (audit_rate = 0.05 for the first weeks). Certification on a uniform sample of live traffic describes your real mix, not just the hard cases the table deferred.

Need Setting
Fewer disagreements overall lower alpha (0.01–0.02)

A floor on confidence, on top of the certificate min_confidence = 0.97 (only ever stricter)
Untrusted input (users trying to game it) exposed = true (also defers answers a few edits could flip)
Try it without risk mode = "shadow", then canary = 0.1, then serve: Rollout
A stricter bound for one question only a [questions.<name>] section with its own alpha

"The table is too big"#

The table holds one small row per word or character pattern it learned, times the number of options, so size grows with vocabulary × options (Scale). Cap it:

intent = shad0w.decision("intent", ..., max_mb=1)       # or SHAD0W_MAX_MB=1, or `shad0w train --max-mb 1`

Training keeps the most informative patterns and refits on them. On 150 options, max_mb=1 gave a 1.00 MB table that certified 94.4% of calibration traffic, the same as the unbudgeted 3.6 MB table (94.4%). Very small budgets on large option sets eventually cost coverage: check the printed certified share.

"My LLM changed"#

The certificate is against the teacher that produced the log. A new model or a new prompt is a new teacher:

  1. Start a fresh log (point log= or folder= at a new place) and keep the old table serving, or switch it to mode = "shadow" to watch how often the new LLM disagrees with it.
  2. Once the new log has enough answers, train(): the new table is certified against the new teacher.

"Should I fine-tune a model instead?"#

They solve different problems and work well together.

Fine-tune a small model shad0w
What you get a smaller model that imitates your LLM a table that answers the cases it can prove it gets right, and defers the rest
Cost to build labelled data or distillation run, GPU hours your LLM's logged answers, seconds to minutes on a CPU
Per decision milliseconds, on a GPU or a fast CPU microseconds, in your process, a browser, or a worker
When it is wrong silently measured: the certificate bounds disagreement; uncertain inputs go to the teacher
Best at tasks that need real language understanding high-volume, recognisable decisions

A common set-up is both: fine-tune a small model as the teacher, then put shad0w in front of it. The table takes the easy majority in microseconds and the fine-tuned model handles the rest. Everything on this page still applies, with the fine-tuned model as the teacher.

Knobs you rarely need#

delta (confidence of the certificate, default 90%), cal_fraction and max_cal (how much of the log certifies), drift_window and drift_margin (how fast the drift guard reacts). Their defaults suit almost everyone; the reference lists them all.
