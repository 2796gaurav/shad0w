Training & retraining#

The log is the training data#

Every time your LLM answers, shad0w appends one line to the question's log:

{"text": "my card was stolen", "intent": "lost_card", "source": "teacher", "ts": 1791370000.1}

That is all training needs: your LLM's own answers. You don't label anything. Answers that don't match an option are never logged, and neither are table answers, so the table never trains on itself.

You can also bring your own logs. Any JSON Lines file with a text field and one field per question works:

{"text": "app crashes on login", "team": "mobile", "urgent": true}

Log the traffic you really get#

The certificate holds for traffic that looks like the log. So the log should be your real traffic, at its real proportions:

  • keep off-topic and odd messages in it, at the rate they really arrive;
  • give every question an other option, so those messages have an answer to learn;
  • don't hand-pick or deduplicate the log into a cleaner set than production.

If off-topic messages are rare in the log but common in production, the table is certified on the wrong mix and can disagree with your LLM more than α. The drift guard flags this kind of shift; retrain when it does. Worked example: CLINC150 in the benchmarks.

When to train#

shad0w stats --log shad0w/intent/log.jsonl

shad0w/intent/log.jsonl: 1,204 answers logged (teacher 1,192, audit 12)

intent: 1,204 answers, 6 different
  lost_card              412  ########################################
  refund                 301  #############################
  ...
  -> ready to train
  • ~1,000 answers gets you started. 100 is the hard minimum.
  • 10k–100k is where the share answered by the table climbs high. In our BANKING77 run it went from 0% at 250 answers to 60% at 10,000.
  • Rare options need examples too. An option with a handful of answers is rarely certified, which is safe: those inputs go to your LLM.

Train#

intent.train()                          # Python: compile + certify + hot-swap
intent.train(alpha=0.02, min_rows=500)  # a stricter bound; try with fewer rows

shad0w train --log shad0w/intent/log.jsonl --out shad0w/intent/bundle      # finds questions and options in the log
shad0w train --dir shad0w --question intent                                # the proxy / decision() folder layout
shad0w train --log log.jsonl --out bundle --schema schema.json --alpha 0.05

A run of 1,000–10,000 answers takes seconds to a minute on a laptop CPU. [compile] installs scipy and scikit-learn. Training uses torch when it is installed (pip install "shad0wllm[torch]"); without it, scipy solves the same objective more slowly on large logs.

The training command:

  1. picks a calibration set: the spot-check rows when there are at least 100 of them (see below), otherwise up to 30% of the answers (at most 3,000), held out at random;
  2. trains the table on the rest;
  3. certifies on the calibration set;
  4. writes manifest.json, one .s0 table per question, certificate.json and schema.json.

The certificate records which calibration set was used: "calibration": "uniform-audit" or "held-out-split".

Why retraining certifies on spot checks#

Before the first table exists, the log holds every call. Afterwards it holds mostly the calls the table deferred: the hard ones. A certificate computed on those would describe a harder mix than your real traffic, which is not what it claims to describe.

So spot checks sample all traffic uniformly. At rate audit_rate, a served answer is re-asked to your LLM in the background, and a deferred answer is marked as a spot check (your LLM answered it anyway). The rows marked "source": "audit" are then a uniform sample of live traffic, and train() certifies on them once there are at least 100.

With fewer, it falls back to a random held-out split of the whole log, and the caveat above applies: the bound then speaks for a harder mix than live traffic. During the first weeks of serving, set audit_rate = 0.05 to collect the sample faster, or run in mode = "shadow", where the table answers nobody and the log stays representative. See Rollout.

Keep it fresh#

Traffic changes. New products, new phrasings and new kinds of users all move the mix. Three ways to stay current:

  • Retrain on a schedule. The log keeps growing with every answer your LLM gives on deferred traffic, plus the spot checks. Retrain weekly: shad0w train .... A running proxy or decision() picks up the new table automatically (or call .reload()).
  • Retrain automatically. auto_train=2000 (Python) or --auto-train 2000 (proxy) retrains in the background every 2,000 new answers. With retrain = "gated" (the default) the new table replaces the current one only if it certifies at least 80% of the current certified share; otherwise the old one stays and a warning is logged. shad0w train --gate does the same from the command line (exit code 3 when it keeps the old table).
  • Re-certify only. shad0w certify --bundle B --data fresh.jsonl keeps the table and refreshes its threshold on fresh traffic it never trained on.

Signals that it is time:

  • the dashboard's live bound creeps toward α;
  • flag="drift" shows up and stays;
  • the share answered by the table drops.
  • the share of other answers (off-topic traffic) moves a lot from what it was when you trained.

Choosing α#

alpha is the most disagreement with your LLM you accept among table answers. The default is 5%. A smaller α means the table answers less, but agrees more.

For example, on BANKING77 with a small open teacher model (bge-small, 68% accurate), the table answered 63.3% of test traffic at α = 5% with 3.3% realised disagreement (the playground table). With gpt-4.1-mini (OpenAI) as the teacher: 0% at α = 1%, 20% at 2%, 61% at 5%, with realised disagreement n/a, 0.4% and 3.0%.

Human labels instead of an LLM#

Have labelled data? Compile from it directly, and certify against the truth instead of your LLM:

shad0w compile   --schema schema.json --data labels.jsonl --out bundle/
shad0w calibrate --bundle bundle/ --data real_labels.jsonl          # ~300 real labels per question
