The guarantee#

In plain words#

Before the table answers anything, shad0w tests it on answers from your LLM that the table never trained on. It looks for a confidence level with this property: whenever the table is at least that confident, it disagrees with your LLM on at most α of the answers it serves (α = 5% by default), with 90% confidence. It allows for bad luck in which answers happened to be held out; that bad luck is what δ measures, so in up to 10% of certifications the realised rate can land above α. In our earlier runs it did on 2 of 7 tasks (3.9–5.7% (above 5% on 2 of 7 tasks; max over seeds 6.5%)); the live spot checks are there to catch that.

That gives you one sentence you can show a reviewer:

On traffic like the calibration traffic, among the answers the table serves, it disagrees with your LLM at most α of the time. This statement is wrong with probability at most δ (10% by default).

Below that confidence level, the table does not answer. Your LLM does.

What it is not#

  • Not a bound against the truth. It compares the table with your LLM. If your LLM is wrong, the table is wrong the same way. For a bound against the truth, calibrate with ~300 human labels (shad0w calibrate).
  • Not valid under any traffic change. It holds for traffic drawn like the calibration traffic. The drift guard, live spot checks and retraining are there for when traffic moves.
  • Not a per-request promise. It is a rate over served answers.

How we keep you honest at runtime#

  • Spot checks. 1% of table answers (audit_rate) are re-asked to your LLM in the background. The dashboard and stats() show the observed disagreement and its 90% upper bound next to α.
  • Drift guard. It needs no labels. shad0w tracks how often confidence falls below a level set at calibration, and when that share rises by 3 points over a 500-decision window, it flags drift and defers until you retrain.

Formally#

Setting. The calibration slice has n texts never used for fitting, with your LLM's answers tᵢ, the table's answers ŷᵢ and confidences cᵢ. Disagreement is eᵢ = 1[ŷᵢ ≠ tᵢ].

Procedure (Learn-then-Test). Sort by confidence. Run two valid tests, each at level δ/2, and keep the more lenient certified threshold. By the union bound, the result is valid at δ.

  1. Fixed-sequence: test growing accepted sets from strict to lenient, and stop at the first whose Clopper–Pearson upper bound exceeds α.
  2. Bonferroni: test 12 coverage levels fixed in advance (5% … 100%), each at δ/24.

Guarantee. With probability ≥ 1 − δ over the calibration sample, P( ŷ(x) ≠ teacher(x) | c(x) ≥ τ* ) ≤ α, for x drawn exchangeably with the calibration data.

Against the truth. If your LLM is right with probability q on served inputs, the served error against the truth is at most α + (1 − q).

A simulation in the test suite draws hundreds of calibration sets from a known population. It checks that the share of runs whose true disagreement exceeds α stays at or below δ, both when disagreement rises as confidence falls and when an LLM-like teacher is wrong a few percent of the time at random.
