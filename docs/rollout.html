Rollout: shadow → canary → serve#

The certificate is a statistical promise made on past traffic. A careful rollout checks it on live traffic before the table answers anyone. Each step below is one line in shad0w.toml (or one keyword, or one environment variable; see Configuration), so moving between steps needs no deploy of new code.

0. Log only#

Before any table exists, every call goes to your LLM and the answer is written to the log. Nothing changes for your users.

shad0w stats --log shad0w/intent/log.jsonl     # how many answers, which options, ready to train?
shad0w train --dir shad0w --question intent    # once ready: build and certify the table

Read the certified share that train prints. If it is close to 0%, the table is not confident enough to help on this question yet. Log more answers, or see Troubleshooting.

1. Shadow#

mode = "shadow"
audit_rate = 0.05      # a larger uniform sample early on: it also makes re-certification honest

The table now decides every call, but your LLM's answer is the one returned. shad0w counts two things:

  • would_serve: calls the table would have answered.
  • shadow_disagreements: of those, how many differed from your LLM.

Read them with intent.stats() in Python, or at /v1/stats and on the dashboard of shad0w proxy / shad0w serve. Deferred calls carry the flag shadow.

Move on when shadow_disagreements / would_serve sits at or below your α on a few thousand calls, and would_serve / all calls is worth having.

2. Canary#

mode = "serve"
canary = 0.1           # the table answers 10% of what it is certified on

Now real users get table answers, but only a tenth of the eligible ones. The rest go to your LLM with the flag canary and keep feeding the log.

Watch the live bound on the dashboard. It is a 90% upper bound on disagreement among served answers, computed from the spot checks. The dashboard shows it green while it is at or below α and amber with "re-train" when it is above.

Move on when the live bound stays at or below α as the number of spot checks grows.

3. Serve#

mode = "serve"
canary = 1.0
audit_rate = 0.01      # keep spot checks on: they are your early warning
auto_train = 1000      # optional: retrain every 1,000 new logged answers
retrain = "gated"      # keep the old table unless the new one is at least as useful

The table answers everything it is certified on. Keep audit_rate above zero. Without spot checks you would only learn about a drop in agreement from your users.

Going back#

Any step can be undone without a restart of your code: set mode = "shadow" or canary = 0 and restart the process that reads the config, or set SHAD0W_MODE=off in its environment. Labels that must never be answered by the table go in never_serve; that works at every step.

What to watch, in one table#

Signal Where Act when
Share answered by the table dashboard, stats()["offload"], /v1/stats It falls: traffic changed. Retrain.
Live bound vs α dashboard, shad0w_audit_disagreement_upper in Prometheus It stays above α. Retrain, or lower canary.
drift flags dashboard "why deferred", stats()["flags"] They appear: inputs look unlike the calibration data. Answers go to your LLM until it clears; retrain on the new traffic.
Log size since last train shad0w stats --log ... It passes min_rows again: a retrain will usually certify more.
